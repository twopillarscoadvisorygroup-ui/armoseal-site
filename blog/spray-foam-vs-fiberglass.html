Spray foam or fiberglass: which is right for my home?

By the Armoseal Insulation team

The short answer

Fiberglass costs less up front and works well in many homes. Spray foam costs more, but it also seals air leaks, which can make a bigger difference in Florida's heat and humidity. The right choice depends on your home and budget.

How fiberglass works

Fiberglass is the pink or yellow insulation most people picture. It comes in rolls (called batts) or as loose pieces that are blown into an attic. It slows down heat moving through your walls and ceiling.

It's affordable and has been used for decades. The catch is that it doesn't stop air from leaking through gaps and cracks, so hot, humid outside air can still sneak in.

How spray foam works

Spray foam is sprayed on as a liquid and expands to fill every gap, crack and corner. Once it hardens, it both insulates and seals air leaks in one step.

That air seal is the big difference. In a hot, humid place like Tampa, keeping outside air out can help your AC work less and keep rooms more even in temperature.

A side-by-side look

FiberglassSpray foam
Up-front costLowerHigher
Seals air leaksNoYes
Fits odd shapes and gapsSomewhatVery well
Good forBudget upgrades, topping up an atticMaximum comfort and energy savings

So which should I pick?

There's no single right answer. Some homes do great with fiberglass. Others get much more comfortable with spray foam. Many people simply want an honest opinion from someone who has looked at their home.
