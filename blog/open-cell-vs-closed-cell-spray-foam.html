Open-cell vs. closed-cell spray foam: what's the difference?

By the Armoseal Insulation team

The short answer

Open-cell foam is softer, lighter and less expensive, and it's often used in attics and walls. Closed-cell foam is denser, insulates more per inch and blocks moisture, but it costs more.

Open-cell spray foam

Open-cell foam is soft and spongy once it hardens. It expands a lot when sprayed, so it fills big spaces well.

  • Insulates roughly R-3.5 to R-3.8 per inch
  • Lower cost than closed-cell
  • Helps quiet outside noise
  • Commonly used under roof decks and in interior walls

Closed-cell spray foam

Closed-cell foam is firm and dense. It packs more insulating power into a thinner layer.

  • Insulates roughly R-6 to R-7 per inch
  • Acts as a moisture barrier
  • Adds strength to the surfaces it's sprayed on
  • Often used where space is tight or moisture is a concern

What does "R-value" mean?

R-value is simply a measure of how well insulation slows down heat. The higher the number, the better it insulates. You can learn more in our article on how much insulation a Florida attic needs.

Which one do I need?

It depends on where it's going and what you want it to do. We'll recommend the right type for each part of your home and explain why, in plain language.
